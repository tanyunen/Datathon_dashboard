1. KEY TEMPORAL FINDINGS

Analysis of 10,000,000 transactions reveals a non-uniform distribution across hours and days.

  • Hourly concentration: 23:00 is the busiest hour (585,888), while 11:00 is the quietest (218,010).
  • Daily concentration: Friday has the highest volume (1,862,981), while Sunday has the lowest (552,588).
  • Peak-hour share: The six busiest hours account for 32.77% of transactions.
  • Weekend activity: Saturday and Sunday account for 14.72% of total volume.

2. RECONSTRUCTED TEMPORAL PATTERNS

The timestamp distribution suggests that transaction records are not evenly distributed across the day or week.

  • Hourly pattern: Transaction counts vary across the 24-hour period, suggesting non-uniform hourly timestamp sampling.
  • Weekday pattern: Transaction volumes differ between weekdays and weekends, suggesting day-related variation.
  • Transaction value: The highest hourly average occurs at 09:00 (USD 159,855.58). This is an association, not proof of causation.

These are reconstructed patterns from observed distributions, not confirmed source-code rules.

3. DATASET GENERATION IMPLICATIONS

The observed temporal patterns provide clues about how the synthetic dataset may have been constructed.

  • The timestamp generator may use weighted hourly sampling instead of uniform sampling.
  • Different weekday and weekend distributions may be incorporated into timestamp generation.
  • Further analysis should compare time patterns with transaction type, currency, participant type and anomaly category.

4. BUSINESS IMPLICATIONS & RECOMMENDATIONS

  • Payment-processing capacity: Use peak periods to guide capacity planning and resource allocation.
  • Operational monitoring: Monitor transaction queues and processing performance during high-volume periods.
  • Time-aware monitoring: Consider normal hourly and weekday patterns when evaluating unusual changes in volume.
  • Intelligent queue management: Prioritise transactions ready for processing while managing those waiting for an available payment route or settlement window.
  • Further analysis: Compare Business and Consumer transaction patterns across hours and weekdays.

5. OVERALL CONCLUSION

Transaction traffic in the synthetic dataset is temporally uneven. Hourly and weekday patterns provide evidence for reconstructing possible timestamp-generation logic and can support operational planning. An Intelligent Time-Aware Payment Queue Management approach could use these patterns to help manage processing queues and reduce potential delays.

Limitation: The dataset is synthetic and fictitious. Route availability, settlement windows and queue status are not included in the temporal analysis. These patterns should not be interpreted as actual global SWIFT payment behaviour.
