Finding, plausible explanation, and limitation

1. Tested question and observed pattern

  • Question: Does average recorded duration differ by route participant count?
  • Mean duration increases from 663 seconds at 2 participants to 841 at 3 and 1,042 at 4.
  • Most records have 2–3 participants; observations become much less common as participant count rises.

2. Plausible business interpretation

Within Indirect routes, mean duration rises about 202 seconds from 3 to 4 participants; within Covered routes it rises about 192 seconds. At 3 participants, Covered has a lower mean than Indirect (810 vs 845 seconds) but a higher median (288 vs 87 seconds). These patterns are consistent with longer routes taking more time, but the mean/median reversal does not support a single fixed Covered-settlement overhead.

3. Limitation

There are 91 records with 13 participants and 43 with 14. Only 21 records have 15 or more participants, spread across groups up to 22. The duration chart focuses on groups 2–14; sparse-tail averages are unstable. Do not infer survivorship bias, failure, compliance outcomes, or liquidity lockup from these fields.

4. Reconstructed rule vs baseline

For recorded routes, mean TOTAL_DURATION is 663 seconds at 2 participants, 841 at 3, and 1,042 at 4. The pooled 2-to-4 difference is 379 seconds. Within-type 3-to-4 differences are 192–202 seconds, but currency, party mix, and long-duration outliers remain uncontrolled; this is descriptive, not a causal per-hop delay.
