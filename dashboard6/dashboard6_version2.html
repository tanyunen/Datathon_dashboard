Analysis Brief

Analysis Method

Each currency contributes one observation. Spearman’s ρ compares its payment volume with its share of payments recording two or more FX steps. The main analysis includes the 21 currencies with at least 50,000 valid payments, giving each currency a larger base for estimating that share.

The all-currency comparison includes very low-volume currencies whose percentages can change sharply with a single payment. For example, SVC’s 75% share represents just three of four payments.

Tested question and results

Question: Does currency usage track with the share of payments recording 2+ FX conversions? Compare all currencies with the higher-volume subset, using the same currency-level method.

Among the 21 eligible currencies, higher usage tends to coincide with a lower 2+ FX share (ρ = −0.55, p ≈ 0.0097). Across all 155 currencies, the relationship is close to zero (ρ = +0.055, p ≈ 0.500). The approximate two-sided tests provide evidence of a negative association within the filtered group.

Reconstructed rule vs baseline

Baseline comparison: all 155 currencies show no clear usage/2+ FX association (ρ = +0.055), while the 21 currencies with at least 50,000 records show a negative association (ρ = −0.55). This pattern applies only to the filtered currency-level comparison; it is not a rule that higher-volume currencies cause fewer conversions. Threshold sensitivity and corridor mix still need checking.

EUR and USD also have higher zero-FX shares (19.85% and 16.49%) than the three lowest-ranked eligible currencies (0.19%–4.23%). To validate the 2+ FX finding, compare currencies within the same corridor, transaction type and participant count, then test whether the result holds at other volume thresholds.

Dataset-wide structural checks

All 10,000,000 rows satisfy CURRENCY_CONVERSION_COUNT ≤ PARTICIPANT_COUNT − 1 (zero violations). Matching sender BIC country codes to their standard ISO currency, 8,938,955 instructions (89.39%) use the sender-country currency. The remaining 1,061,045 selections are not explained by observed fields; the data does not establish account denomination or when and where FX occurs. “90%” is a rounded summary.
