Comprehensive Economic & Technical Analytical Brief

1. Tested question and finding

Question: Do sender types differ in normalized payment amount? Business senders account for 7,874,637 of 10,000,000 records (78.75%) and have a $5,537.36 median versus $772.62 for Consumer senders (7.17×).

2. Party-pair finding

Business-to-Consumer (B → C) is the largest pair, with 5,603,692 records (56.04%). Median normalized amounts are $22,887.71 for B → B and $463.17 for C → C (about 49.4×). This descriptive contrast is not adjusted for currency or route and does not establish payment purpose; payroll or payout explanations cannot be confirmed here.

3. Reconstructed rule

Reconstructed rule vs baseline: Among the 10 million records, Business senders (n=7,874,637) have a median normalized amount of $5,537.36 versus the Consumer baseline (n=2,125,363) at $772.62—7.17× higher. This is descriptive and does not define an operational hold or routing threshold.

4. Limitation and next question

The data does not establish payment purpose, account balances, batch schedules, or real-world liquidity demand. A useful next test is whether the sender-type amount difference persists within comparable currencies and routing types.

Evidence-led business questions

1. Segment payment monitoring

Compare payment amounts by sender type within the same currency and transaction type before designing different review thresholds.

2. Validate payment purpose

Use transaction-purpose data, if available and permitted, before attributing B → C payments to payroll, payouts, or other use cases.
