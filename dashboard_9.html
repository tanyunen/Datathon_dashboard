Question and reconstructed rule

Question: Does the share of records with a listed ANOMALY_TYPE label differ across CURRENCY_CODE groups?

Rule: For each currency with n >= 50,000, divide rows with a non-empty anomaly label by all records in that currency, then scale to 10,000 records. Compare with the 21 eligible groups' pooled baseline.

What the data shows

INR has the highest observed rate among eligible groups: 87.954 per 10,000 (450 of 51,163), versus 6.074 per 10,000 overall in the eligible groups. This is a descriptive difference, not evidence that the currency caused an anomaly.

Interpretation: The difference can guide a follow-up check of label definitions and currency mix. It does not show currency errors, fraud losses, liquidity shortages, or operational risk.

Method and limits: 10,000,000 records had a currency code; 0 had none. The 21 shown groups cover 8,714,194 records; smaller groups are excluded to avoid unstable rates. Labels are dataset annotations, not confirmed real-world incidents. Currency, amount, route, and transaction-type mix are not controlled here.

Source: supplied fictitious payment dataset. Generated by generate_dashboards9_10.py. Rates are descriptive and apply only to this dataset.
