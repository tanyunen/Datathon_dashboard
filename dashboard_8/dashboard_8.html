Observed pattern, interpretation, and limits

1. Tested question and result

Question: How do the six displayed anomaly-label groups compare with other records? Of 10,000,000 records, 7,006 have one of those labels (0.0701%); the other 9,992,994 form the baseline. Romance scam is most frequent (2,476). Fat-finger records have a $260,241.23 median normalized amount versus $3,662.58 for the baseline.

2. Reconstructed rule

For records assigned one of the six displayed ANOMALY_TYPE labels, group by label and compare counts and median USD_AMOUNT with the records outside those label groups. Fat-finger-labelled records have a median about 71× the other-record baseline. This is a descriptive comparison, not a confirmed fraud-loss estimate.

3. Business interpretation

Higher amounts in a category may justify closer review, but amount alone does not establish a loss, fraud success, or preventability.

4. Sequence and graph signatures

  • Among 1,976 same-sender/beneficiary Romance Scam transitions, 1,920 (97.2%) are within 18 minutes and 1,642 (83.1%) increase in normalized USD amount; the median next/previous ratio is 1.50, not a rule for every transfer.
  • Duplicate Payment has 494 exact repeated normalized-USD transitions; the median interval is 1,099 seconds (18.3 minutes), and 242 of 494 are within 18 minutes.
  • Seven CEO-scam beneficiary accounts and three account-replacement beneficiary accounts each have at least 7 distinct sender accounts; the maxima are 10 and 13, respectively.
  • 1,938 of 1,939 Fat-finger sender/beneficiary pairs occur once; all 317 Customer Recall pairs occur once. Their trigger is not observable.

5. Method and limitation

Scope: Sequence checks use labelled rows grouped by account pair and compare USD_AMOUNT, not raw-currency amounts. These are synthetic labels and normalized values; the dataset does not provide confirmed losses, account balances, or intervention outcomes.

6. Practical follow-up

  • Compare labelled and unlabelled records using party type, route, time, and amount distributions.
  • Show sample sizes alongside each category comparison.
  • Describe prevention ideas as recommendations to investigate, not effects proven by this dataset.
